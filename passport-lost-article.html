Lost Your Jamaican Passport Overseas?

By Docu-Hub Operations • Estimated Read: 3 mins

Losing a passport while living or traveling overseas is an incredibly stressful experience. Whether it slipped out of your bag on the subway or was stolen along with your wallet, your first priority must be protecting your identity.

The Passport, Immigration and Citizenship Agency (PICA) treats a lost passport as a serious security issue. You cannot simply apply for a renewal online; you must follow a strict legal protocol to ensure the missing passport is flagged globally.

Step 1: The Mandatory Police Report

The very first thing you must do—before you even print out a new application—is file a report with your local police precinct in your current country (US, UK, Canada, etc.).

Why is this required?

PICA requires an official Police Report to verify that the document is truly lost or stolen. This report allows them to invalidate the electronic chip and flag the passport number with INTERPOL, ensuring no one can use your identity to travel illegally.

Step 2: Securing Your Application Materials

Because you no longer have your passport to prove your identity, replacing a lost book requires more rigorous documentation than a standard renewal. You will need:

The PICA Application: You must completely fill out a new application form, paying special attention to the section that asks you to detail exactly how and where the passport was lost.

Proof of Identity: You must submit your Original Jamaican Birth Certificate, along with a valid government-issued photo ID from your current country of residence (such as a Driver's License or State ID).

Penalty Fees: Be prepared that PICA charges a higher penalty fee to replace a lost or stolen passport compared to a standard, physical renewal.

Step 3: Filing the Proxy Application

Because the online portal cannot process lost passports, the application and police report must be physically submitted. However, you do not need to book an emergency flight to Kingston.
