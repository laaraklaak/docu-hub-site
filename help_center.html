Case Study #001

The "Ghost" Ticket: Why You Need a Clearance Check.

A client walked into the Tax Office for a routine renewal and walked out with a court summons. Here is how we fixed it.

The Scenario

Mr. Campbell (Client Ref: DH-902) attempted to renew his Driver’s License in Spanish Town. The cashier informed him that his record was blocked due to an "Unexecuted Warrant" from 2019. He had no memory of the incident.

Risk Level

High Risk: Arrest possible at traffic stops.

The Disconnection Problem

Many drivers assume that if they have their receipt, they are safe. However, in Jamaica, the Tax Administration (TAJ) database and the Traffic Court (CMS) database do not always sync instantly.

Mr. Campbell had paid a ticket in 2019 via a third-party app. The payment cleared at the Tax Office, but the "Settled" status never triggered the removal of the court date. When he didn't show up for court (because he thought he paid), a warrant was automatically issued.

The Anatomy of the Error

Feb 2019

Ticket Issued

Speeding violation ($5,000).

Mar 2019

Online Payment Made

Receipt generated, but Court system not notified.

Apr 2019

Warrant Issued

Judge issues Bench Warrant for non-appearance.

Today

Docu-Hub Intervention

Reconciliation of records & license renewed.

The Docu-Hub Solution

Instead of sending Mr. Campbell to court (which requires a lawyer and a day off work), we initiated our Traffic Ticket Reconciliation Service.

  • Step 1: We retrieved the payment history from the TAJ portal using his TRN.
  • Step 2: Our liaison physically took the stamped receipt to the Court Registry office.
  • Step 3: The clerk manually updated the CMS database to "Satisfied."
  • Step 4: The block was lifted, and we renewed the license the same afternoon.
