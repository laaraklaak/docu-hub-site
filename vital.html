Which process applies to you?

Standard Record Search

Select this option if you simply need official certified printed copies of an existing, perfectly correct record:

  • Birth Certificates: Securing fresh, certified copies for immigration or personal files.
  • Marriage Certificates: Retrieving records of marriages registered in Jamaica.
  • Death Certificates: Official documentation for estate settling and insurance claims.
  • Genealogy Lookups: Manual archival searches for historical family documents.

Record Corrections & Updates

Select this option if a record requires legal amendment or updating before it can be printed:

  • Father's Particulars: Adding a father's name to an existing birth certificate.
  • Correction of Error: Fixing spelling mistakes in names, dates, or locations on the original ledger.
  • Late Registration: Registering a birth or death that was never officially documented at the time.

Educational Scenario

Why it matters if a Father's Name is missing on a Birth Certificate

It is not uncommon in Jamaica for a child to be registered without the father’s name on the official birth certificate, or for a name to be slightly misspelled. For day-to-day life on the island, this might never cause an issue.

If you discover this omission on your record, there is absolutely no reason to panic. It is not a permanent roadblock; it is simply an administrative gap that can be addressed and fixed.

When does this become important?

Addressing this becomes critical primarily for Jamaicans living in the diaspora. If you plan to sponsor a family member for immigration, or if you need to claim an inheritance overseas, foreign governments and estate lawyers require irrefutable, documentary proof of blood lineage. In these legal scenarios, an incomplete RGD birth certificate can halt your application.

"A missing name or a misspelling is a common issue that has a clear, established legal remedy at the Registrar General's Department."

The Solution: The RGD has specific processes known as the Addition of Father's Particulars or Correction of Error. It involves submitting specific statutory declarations to amend the original ledger. Once the ledger is updated, a new, fully correct certificate is printed.

Learn More: Who needs to sign the declarations?
To add a father's name to a birth certificate, the RGD requires specific declarations depending on the situation:
  • If both parents are alive: A joint statutory declaration signed by both the mother and the father acknowledging paternity.
  • If the father is deceased: A declaration from the mother, supported by a Death Certificate and corroborating affidavits from older paternal relatives.
  • If parents were married: A certified copy of the Marriage Certificate can be used to legitimize the birth entry.
