Important: The Wet Signature Workflow

The Ministry of Labour and Social Security requires official, witnessed original documents to process claims. We do not simply email you a blank form to figure out on your own. To ensure your claim is not rejected by the agency, we follow a strict preparation protocol:

1

Digital Preparation: We collect your details online and professionally type your complex NIS forms to ensure exact MLSS compliance.

2

Print & Witness: We email you the completed PDF. You must print it and sign it (wet signature) in the presence of a Notary Public.

3

Physical Dispatch: You must place the signed original forms in a courier envelope (e.g., DHL) and mail them to our Jamaica office.

4

In-Person Proxy: We receive your physical documents and submit them by hand directly to the NIS office on your behalf.

Educational Scenario

Retiring Overseas: Claiming Your Jamaican Pension

Thousands of Jamaicans currently living in the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada spent the first 10 to 15 years of their careers working in Jamaica. During that time, National Insurance Scheme (NIS) contributions were deducted from their paychecks.

Decades later, when it comes time to retire, many people assume that because they migrated, that money is simply gone. Alternatively, they know they have an NIS number, but are completely unsure of how many contributions they have or how to initiate a claim from abroad.

You are likely entitled to a benefit.

If you made contributions while working in Jamaica, those records still exist. Depending on how many weeks of contributions were logged, you may be eligible for a recurring Retirement Pension or a one-time Retirement Grant.

"Calling the NIS offices from overseas to track down decades-old employment records is incredibly difficult. Most older records require a manual, physical ledger search."

The Solution: You do not need to fly back to Jamaica to trace your history. The process begins with a formal Record Retrieval to pull your Contribution History Statement. Once we know exactly where your account stands, you can safely submit your claim.

Learn More: What is needed for a Funeral Grant?
If an active NIS contributor passes away, the family can claim a Funeral Grant. However, the documentation must be exact:
  • The official Death Certificate of the contributor.
  • The official Receipt from the Funeral Home (showing the applicant's name).
  • Proof of relationship (e.g., Marriage Certificate or Birth Certificate).
  • The deceased’s NIS card and the applicant's valid ID.

Docu-Hub assists families overseas in preparing these exact documents to ensure the grant is paid without unnecessary delay.
